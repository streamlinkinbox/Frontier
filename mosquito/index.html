Field Guide · Aedes aegypti ♀
Every parameter in this rig traces to published research — no faked motion.

1 · Morphology (female)

4–7 mm dark mosquito; white lyre marking on scutum; white knee spots; pale basal bands on tarsi with hind tarsomere 5 mostly white; pointed abdomen tip with cerci; short maxillary palps with white tips; pilose antennae (13 flagellomeres, short whorl hairs — males are plumose); kidney-shaped compound eyes; single wing pair with vein scales + trailing fringe; halteres; pretarsus with paired claws, pulvilli and empodium.

ECDC reverse ID key 2022 · AZDHS identification guide (Ramberg) · Supriyono et al., Biodiversitas · Cambridge “Introduction to mosquitoes (Culicidae)”

2 · Flight — low-amplitude, high-frequency

Stroke amplitude ≈ 39–44° (lowest of any hovering animal), wingbeat ≈ 550–600 Hz (♀) to 700–800 Hz (♂), figure-eight wingtip path from large deviation angles, stroke plane pitched ≈ 47.5° to the body axis. Lift comes largely from rotational mechanisms + trailing-edge vortex at stroke reversal — not helicopter-like sweeping. Hind legs trail extended, stabilising roll. Blood/fuel load raises stroke amplitude, rotation amplitude and frequency.

Bomphrey et al., Nature 2017 · Muijres et al., J. Exp. Biol. 2017 (JEB163402) · Tran, PhD thesis (Leeds) · Bomphrey/WhiteRose aerodynamics

3 · Feeding — fascicle in, labium out

The labium is only a sheath: it buckles back and stays on the surface while the 6-stylet fascicle (2 serrated maxillae = micro-saws, 2 mandibles, hypopharynx = saliva canal, labrum = food canal) penetrates with ~16 µN force. Saliva first (anticoagulant), labrum steers to a vessel, then the cibarial pump engorges the midgut to ~3× body mass. Stretch receptors trigger withdrawal. Excess water is excreted by diuresis mid-meal.

Kong & Wu, Phys. Rev. E 2010 · Choumet et al. 2012 · PNAS 2022 (DENV feeding) · Frontiers in Physiology 2015 · AskNature / Wootton

4 · Walking — a gait continuum, never snapping

No fixed gears: wave → tetrapod → tripod blends continuously with speed (duty factor 0.8 → 0.5). Tripod pairs are (L1·R2·L3) ⇄ (R1·L2·R3). Stance feet stay world-anchored while the body travels over them; swing feet follow minimum-jerk lift trajectories into restricted workspaces, so legs can never cross or pop. Uphill / loaded walking shifts back toward tetrapod–wave.

Graham 1972/1985 · Cruse et al., Walknet (Biol. Cybern. 2013) · Bender et al. 2011 · JEB 215:4255 (stick-insect slopes)

5 · Takeoff & landing

Wings spin up ~30 ms before liftoff; long legs extend slowly and push with only ~27% of a fruit fly's peak force — wings supply ≥60–100% of liftoff force, so escape is near-silent. Last leg off is usually a middle leg (57%). Landing flares, legs reach, wings fold scissor-like over the abdomen.

Muijres, Chang et al., JEB 2017 / UC Berkeley 2017 · Bomphrey et al. 2017

6 · Walls & ceilings — claws + pulvilli

Tarsal claws interlock micro-roughness; on smooth surfaces the pulvilli (setae pads + oily secretion) adhere via capillary / van der Waals forces. Grip is angle-dependent: the rig flattens the tarsus and spreads pulvilli on smooth metal, and hooks claws on rough rock. Gait is identical in the surface frame — “down” is just the surface normal.

Max Planck (Stuttgart) adhesion studies · Sci. American insect-adhesion review · Cambridge Culicidae morphology (pretarsus)
