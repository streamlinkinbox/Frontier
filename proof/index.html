Moving mesh, moving distance field

Fixed camera. The body moves 2.42 m along world X between the endpoints; the wheels turn. All three animations follow the same 12 poses. The second panel is an XZ section read from the near world volume, not an outline drawn from the mesh. Orange = negative distance, cream = near zero, blue = positive. GIF version (palette quantized). The PNG animation is the colour reference.

Not production-ready: the raw GI visibly stipples the floor and body. The white front light panel loses detail. No clean-frame or photorealism claim.

02 / representation

Baked distances and surface identity

R16F object-space distance textures stay fixed. The C++ baker stores the nearest authored body-triangle material in a separate R8UI texture. The moving union shader reads that ID instead of guessing glass, paint and lamps from XYZ. These flat ID views are diagnostics, not shaded renders.

Area-weighted material disagreement: 81.57% → 7.29%. Lamp/non-lamp disagreement: 2.39% → 0.38%. Thin boundaries are still quantized. The cache is not a full Unreal-style surface-card system.

03 / visibility

One sun, nine visibility estimates

White is fully visible, black is occluded or back-facing. These are the unfiltered red channels of the shadow targets, not recoloured beauty images. The independent reference casts 512 triangle rays per eligible receiver over the same 2.4° solar cap. It is a numerical reference, not a photograph.

On 13,594 partial-visibility pixels, the packet reduces visibility RMSE from 0.05033 to 0.02020 versus the 16-ray scalar cap. At equal sample count, packet and scalar RGBA match bit-for-bit. See measured warm draw times; these exclude the rest of the frame.

04 / radiance

Which source lights the cache?

Full-resolution unfiltered cone gather (incident diffuse irradiance divided by π, approximated by the lab). No camera-visible emission, direct BRDF, background, portal or sky fill in these AOVs. Same exposure throughout this row set: Reinhard then exact sRGB transfer. Geometry and material colour stay present in every case.

Deterministic is not noise-free. The 14-cone screen-space rotation leaves a visible sampling pattern. A denoiser would conceal it, not validate it.

04b / indirect only

What bounced off the ground?

Separate CPU path integral: source → diffuse floor → visible receiver. No direct illumination, self-emission, sky or other bounce types. Black ground is intentional: a planar floor cannot illuminate itself. Each receiver gathers 1,024 deterministic cosine directions from a 257 × 193 world-space floor radiance cache. Same ×8 display exposure for all six. This bounded 16 × 12 m floor is not the entire scene and is not DDGI.

Linear energy and cache/gather timings. The white and red contributions on opposite sides in the emission test originate from the actual headlamp and taillamp triangles.

05 / cross-check

Emission: guessed versus measured

Matched exposure ×4 for every image below. The last two integrate the mesh emitter → receiver segment for a unit diffuse receiver: this is direct illumination from emissive geometry, not proof of a second diffuse bounce. The reference uses 64 samples on each authored emitting triangle with Embree visibility.

The sharp silhouette in the reference is expected: the AOV does not add camera-visible lamp emission. Centroid quadrature greatly reduces the floor error, but still misses some near-field occlusion.
