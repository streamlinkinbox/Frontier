# Decision: keep this in the lab

The native shadow packet passes the matched-pose quality/speed test. The **whole renderer does not**. Do not replace Frontier's Vulkan renderer with this experiment. No hardware-GPU frame rate, zero-artifact result, or photographic ground truth was obtained.

## What ran

The active `streamlinkinbox/Frontier` base (`4a509612`) contained only a README. We fetched [the requested source revision](https://github.com/SultanAladin/Frontier-1/tree/c205cb698288ccd90bf909e25e28ce28d48d0dfa) without switching branches. It contains `Experimental/ClothEditor`, `Experimental/ShadowAlgorithmLab`, `Experimental/DeterministicPathTracer`, and a separate `Experimental/SdfLightingDemoCpp` showcase. The latter builds a primitives scene, not the ControlVehicle. Its default configs enable GI bilateral filtering and path-tracer à-trous reconstruction. Its `RenderEngine::SdfGi` uses **contact PCSS** for these demo shadows. It is not a native port of the vehicle labs. Its path integrator even uses a constant 0.35 as an emissive-hit weight in some paths (`RendererEngines.hpp`, `tracePathTracerPixel`); that is not a computed MIS weight.

* **Scene:** actual FVB1 bundle, 4 mesh BLASes, 9,240 stored triangles, 5 baked volumes. Ten instances: body, four rims, four tyres and arena. No substitute meshes, images in the lighting path, remeshing or per-pose geometry rebaking. The mesh/mask reference intentionally matches the source's glass-skipping shadow policy, not physical glass transmission.
* **Browser:** WebGL 2 shadow lab adapted in scratch; full 480×270 for geometry, shadow and GI (upstream GI normally traces at reduced resolution); highest original quality preset, 14 cones. Shadow filtering disabled, 3-pass GI bilateral removed, final GI fetched at the exact pixel. No denoiser. Composite uses exact sRGB encoding after the lab's filmic curve. Albedo/BRDF/radiance operations are linear. Mask images are **linear visibility codes**; false colours in distance/ID views are not radiometric values.
* **C++:** the C++17 baker builds the body material cache. A native EGL/GLES runner compiles and executes the actual fullscreen passes, binding their uniform arrays, instance transforms, 2D and 3D textures. Native vs browser raw FP readbacks were byte-identical for local SDF, near/far SDF, hybrid, Sobol BVH and cone GI. The included archive has **pass inputs**, not output pictures. This is a frozen-pass interface: native C++ is **not** driving the live scene, world union, WebGPU tracer or a Vulkan swapchain.
* **Path tracer:** ran the original studio megakernel in Chromium 143 WebGPU, full res, `trace=1`, no history, no reconstruction, shared pattern. Actual linear accumulation digests: `785f46d6` (1 spp), `3fdb0e98` (16), `378a54f3` (64). Fresh 1/16 launches repeated the hashes. No nonfinite accumulation components in these captures. The studio lighting and camera differ; **do not** compute an RMSE between the studio and shadow lab. The studio has finite bounce/depth and throughput cutoffs; its night mode is not strict emissive isolation.
* **Execution:** 2 vCPUs, Intel Xeon @ 2.60 GHz, software Vulkan (SwiftShader) through ANGLE; no `/dev/dri`. Shadow lab and native tests used the SwiftShader shipped with `@sparticuz/chromium@133.0.0`. Some drivers may compile or round differently; same-device repeats are the determinism test.

## Signed distances, motion and cache

Local R16F body (88×64×36), rim (28×40×40), tyre (32×44×44) textures retain the source precision. Two fixed world cascades (144×128×64, 128×96×48) cover different extents. **These are not camera-scrolling clipmaps.** The lab shades static radiance into RGBA16F and unions transformed local SDFs into the near cascade: RGB = radiance, A = signed distance; reflectivity is separate R16F. The far cascade stays static. GI samples these radiance and distance channels while marching. This is a voxel radiance cache, not a full surface-card cache or a probe/DDGI solver. DDGI is a different mode in the upstream C++ showcase.

One concrete defect: the original union chooses glass and lamp materials by testing XYZ ranges. The prototype's nearest-triangle body cache is 202,752 bytes of R8UI, point-sampled (never interpolate material IDs). The C++ baker searches the actual triangle BLAS at each voxel center using an AABB lower bound and point/triangle distance. The body and cache share one rigid inverse transform. Sixty-four deterministic points per triangle, weighted by triangle area, give this comparison against authored IDs:

| Error on body surface | Coordinate rule | Baked triangle material |
|---|---:|---:|
| Any wrong material | 81.57% | 7.29% |
| Wrong lamp/non-lamp classification | 2.39% | 0.38% |

The cache is **still an approximation**. It cannot recover subvoxel lamps or seams. Wheel normals in the upstream union are radial from the **car centre**, not the wheel SDF gradient. The union truncates the body lookup region, and the SDF marcher uses a fixed iteration budget and large normal bias. The raw masks show lost wheel/contact detail. For a general scene: retain material/normal correspondence at bake time, transform true local gradients, track dirty old **and** new bounds in every affected cascade, and use triangle visibility where thin shells cannot be represented. The packet test is evidence for the last step; it does not implement the rest.

Motion uses 12 absolute poses (`jobs/motion.json`), X −1.20 to +1.22 m, Y=0.16X, yaw 0.16 rad, wheel angle X/0.50855. Fixed camera, Z-up rigid transforms, no temporal history. The captured XZ slice at the centre Y cell of the near volume moves with the mesh while the fixed wall and ground stay put. Slice views display nearest cells at equal metric scale; 480×270 is the **display** size, not the voxel resolution. `emissive-motion.png` is a separate unfiltered emission-only capture through the same poses. `drive.png` is true-colour APNG; `drive.gif` is palette-quantized. Neither playback speed is a performance measurement.

## Shadow result — better and faster for one measured pass

The original lab's BVH shadow sampler uses a golden-angle disk pattern, not Sobol spherical-cap sampling. The test implements two Sobol dimensions from Gray-code direction numbers, linear interpolation in cos(theta), and uniform azimuth. Baseline: 16 rays, one BVH traversal each. Candidate: 32 rays with a common origin, one conservative cone/AABB packet walk of the instance hierarchy and local BLASes. Exact per-lane triangle tests preserve nearest blockers. On this pose the candidate's **entire RGBA buffer equals the separate-traversal 32-ray result**, SHA-256 `95d56e074b5b8411f7328f818493418cf363338dac568bf869668a0f1b4ead2a`. Stack capacity is for the pinned balanced hierarchies, not a general unbounded production scene.

Independent comparison: Embree, 512 equal-solid-angle Fibonacci directions/receiver, same sun, receiver, backface cutoff, ray interval, glass exclusion and bias. No spatial filtering or denoised reference.

| Visibility pass | Full-image linear RMSE | RMSE on 13,594 partially visible pixels |
|---|---:|---:|
| Local SDF | 0.07425 | 0.14447 |
| Near/far SDF | 0.07342 | 0.16120 |
| Hybrid contact + local SDF | 0.04395 | 0.12025 |
| 16 separate Sobol rays | 0.01630 | 0.05033 |
| 32-ray packet | **0.00654** | **0.02020** |

Median warmed native draw: **1,893.15 ms → 601.51 ms (3.15×)**. Identical 480×270 receiver buffers; the faster pass uses **twice** the samples. Six timed draws per method in forward/reverse blocks after a warm-up; `steady_clock`, `glFinish()` at the timed boundary, no asset loads or readback inside. All samples in `benchmark.json`. This measures only this sun visibility pass on software GLES, **not** whole-frame speed or target-device GPU throughput.

## What the light-isolation images mean

1. **Voxel cone AOV** (`emission`, `point`, `sun`, `area`, `all`, `null`): isolate which sources feed cached radiance, then read the unfiltered cone target, not the composite. Switch off all background/portal/sky terms for these tests. 1 = vehicle materials 12/13/14, 2 = sun, 4 = inverse-square point, 8 = one-sided rectangle. Rectangle injection uses 2×2 deterministic quadrature. Cache visibility is approximate and some bounce/occlusion weights are inherited heuristics. Off produced exactly zero RGB; repeating the emission pose reproduced the float hash `030c334c0e26798980d80c5b2200109fdacde7a8d85decef098e9061a4896803`. The regular dotted pattern is real, not compression noise.
2. **Mesh-emitter transfer** (`*-transfer`): the light leaving an emissive triangle and reaching the receiver directly is **direct light**, even if a GI gather encountered it. Compare that component against a separate Embree integral over the real emitting triangles (64 stratified points per triangle). One-sided Lambertian emitter, white diffuse receiver, cosine at both ends, inverse-square distance, segment visibility, no sky or scene lights. In the 63,771 ground pixels the linear RMSE vs that reference is **0.19169** with heuristic IDs, **0.07248** with baked IDs, **0.000614** with native mesh-emitter centroid quadrature. Reference quadrature has finite error: the independent 16-point rule has RMSE 0.000162 there. This does not certify all BRDFs or more bounces. Native mesh transfer 2,361 ms vs cone gather 2,369 ms median is within timing scatter: **no GI speedup claim**.
3. **Genuinely indirect, floor-only AOV** (`bounce-*`): emitter/point/sun/rectangle → neutral matte floor patch → receiver. Zero camera-visible emission or direct receiver lighting. Compute incident irradiance at 257×193 world-space cache nodes over a 16×12 m floor; multiply by floor reflectance/π; gather using 1,024 cosine-distributed Sobol directions per eligible receiver with independent triangle visibility on both segments. Mesh emitters use 16 points/face; rectangle uses 64; sun uses 64 cap directions. This is a CPU reference for **one bounded path class**, not full radiosity or DDGI. No screen-space denoiser. Off is a separately gathered zero-radiance channel. The combined channel was gathered after summing sources in the cache, not composed from PNGs; max linear discrepancy from the separate results is 1.14e-6. The light floor does not illuminate itself; black ground is correct in this indirect-only view. Cache resolution, surface interpolation, 4 mm origin offset and angular quadrature remain approximations. Colours are scene-linear RGB, not spectrally calibrated real-world photometry.

## Visual and systems findings

I viewed the generated images. In the moving composite the car and shadows move, but the red wall, paint and floor have a pronounced stipple and the broad white front panel clips (4,722 of 388,800 RGB channel values reach 255). The isolated triangle-backed lamp transfer gives a smooth pale pool in front and red illumination behind, considerably narrower than the coordinate-box cone estimate. The floor-bounce-only AOV shows red and white indirect illumination on opposite sides with no lit sky. The ID cache still has stair steps at material boundaries. The 64-spp studio shot retains dark structured streaks in the reflected highlights. It is not evidence of noise-free convergence. Raw shadow/GI/slice checks, including NaN/Inf and display extrema, are in `checks.json`; a finite buffer is not a visual-quality pass.

* The lab's default shaded composite adds portal light, fallback ambient, screen-space AO, a fitted highlight and empirical sky reflections. It is **not** an energy-conserving reference BRDF. Nonlinear cone opacity and clipping can violate light superposition. Source isolation bypasses the beauty composite; exact sRGB transfer does not fix transport errors.
* Packed geometry uses 32-byte BLAS nodes and 64-byte triangle records, checked in C++; FP32 BVH coordinates and indices stored as exactly representable floats for this asset. Raster guides use FP16 world position and normals: adequate for this comparison, not proof against thin-feature loss or large-world cancellation. Normals/coverage, depth bias and glass transmission need dedicated references.
* Near-union redraw still covers 64 full slices per moving pose. Source light changes can trigger full-volume re-shading and two radiosity propagations. Native replay uploads once, then draws; CPU synchronization, texture dumps and readbacks are **validation only**. GPU memory lifetimes, Vulkan hazards, transient aliasing, descriptor binding, swapchain present modes and frame pacing were not verified in the engine. The original browser loop clamps elapsed time before its FPS accounting; its HUD is not a pacing measurement.
* A 60 Hz frame has 16.67 ms; 120 Hz has 8.33 ms. No discrete GPU was available, and no result here establishes either lock. Before integrating: use a shared matched-scene integrator for multi-bounce energy, fine/outer-cascade transitions and moving-light invalidation; compare on moving sequences without denoising; measure complete GPU submissions and presented frame-time percentiles on the intended hardware. Do not port the noisy cone estimator unchanged.
