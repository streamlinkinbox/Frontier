Reference notebook.

One species. A consistent body plan.

Proportions follow an adult female European mantis: narrow pronotum, triangular head capsule, two raptorial forelegs, four supporting legs, and two pairs of folded wings.

A walk, not a claim of motion capture.

The slow walk uses the middle and hind legs in a rear-to-front wave, keeping the grasping forelegs folded. Swing and planted-foot velocity match a 2.5 mm/s controller speed. The scrolling grid is a tracking-view aid; the GLB loop remains in-place. Mantises also use other gaits, including foreleg-assisted locomotion.

The “karate” posture.

Stance is a held defensive display: raised forelegs expose the inner coxal markings, the prothorax becomes more upright, and the wing covers lift over pale, veined hindwing fans. It is separate from the feeding strike.

A controllable hunting sequence.

Target test adds an authored visual-fixation and slow-lean sequence before a fast, target-aware strike. Both grippers are solved to the bead while the four supporting feet remain planted. The strike commits to an aim point; moving the bead away can cause a miss. This is a behavior prototype, not a claim of biological vision or measured timing.

A strike, not a punch.

The revised attack holds the femur folded during the coxal approach, then sweeps and clamps in one fast movement. Closed grippers pull back towards the mouth; the supporting feet stay planted. It is a reference-informed animation, not a motion-capture recording.
