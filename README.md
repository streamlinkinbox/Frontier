# Frontier · WebGPU SDF GI

A standalone browser experiment for a **dynamic signed-distance global illumination pipeline**. It is implemented in WebGPU/WGSL and does not call the native C++ SDF code.

## Run the exhibit

Serve this repository over `localhost` or HTTPS, then open `Exhibits/DistanceFieldGI/`:

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8080 --bind 0.0.0.0
# http://localhost:8080/Exhibits/DistanceFieldGI/
```

Use a current WebGPU-capable browser. The exhibit intentionally fails with a readable message rather than silently falling back to WebGL.

## What it does

- Loads the **ControlVehicle GLB** from the linked Frontier project. Its asset manifest identifies the mesh as byte-identical to the Project Drive-authored vehicle geometry and materials.
- Builds a stackless triangle BVH in JavaScript, then bakes a signed, material-carrying object-distance volume on the GPU using closest-triangle distance and per-part ray parity.
- Maintains three voxel-snapped **64³ global distance-field clipmaps** around the vehicle-following camera target. Every triangle in the streamed static-world render mesh is serialized with its material and card identity into a stackless BVH; each updated voxel traverses that BVH for nearest-triangle distance, oriented sign, albedo, roughness, metallic, emission, and surface/card metadata. The default 5×5 city neighborhood contains about 66,000 static triangles, and the count follows streamed geometry rather than the separate 55-shape analytic budget. The stream worker builds/transfers both mesh and BVH buffers off the render thread, including after emitter-size changes. Dirty cascades update round-robin; toroidal addressing reuses overlapping voxels and recomputes exposed cells, while the moving vehicle remains a separate live SDF query. The 55 analytic shapes remain only as a temporary not-yet-ready fallback. GI secondary hits recover mesh material from the GDF's packed surface ID and material volumes, using world-space mesh-card parameters where available; the fine-field debug slice displays the sampled mesh field rather than replacing it with analytic corrections. Overlapping cascades smoothly blend distance and voxel scale. The optional **Cascade influence** view color-codes surfaces by the fine/middle/coarse blend; labelled x-ray wireframe bounds and the live fine-cascade slice remain available for inspection.
- Generates a deterministic, streamed **100,000 m × 10,000 m** city world from seeded 128 m blocks. A 3×3 neighborhood is rebuilt as the car crosses block boundaries, with intersecting roads, sidewalks, buildings and windows, nine parked cars, four emissive-visored pedestrian robots, and six streetlight emitters. Only nearby geometry is rasterized; the player can pass through city props, while the world perimeter keeps the drive bounded.
- Renders the actual GLB vehicle into a world-space G-buffer and streams a dense 5×5 neighborhood of 96 m city blocks across the 100 km × 10 km world. On a cell crossing, a module worker builds the next neighborhood off the render thread; its vertex buffer is transferred and uploaded once, avoiding synchronous city meshing during driving. Streets have asphalt, sidewalks, lane markings, and crosswalks; blocks contain varied multi-storey buildings with window grids, setbacks, balconies, roof plant, and storefront details, plus parked cars, traffic lights, streetlights, benches, chairs, bins, and globally placed emissive-visor robots. World-cell layouts, including parked-car and robot placements, are keyed by global coordinates and remain stable when chunks are revisited; the 3×3 center retains a fixed 55-shape analytic fallback budget, which no longer limits static GDF coverage. The four named tyre meshes preserve their own part IDs; each rolls by travelled distance divided by its measured GLB radius, and the front pair steers with the car. WASD/arrow steering uses a speed-sensitive bicycle model with forward and reverse gears; drag orbits around the car and the wheel zooms. Optional straight auto-cruise is off by default. The renderer resolves distance-field visibility, AO, environment specular, and cosine-weighted diffuse paths with up to three bounces. Primary visibility is produced by depth-tested triangle rasterization into four full-precision G-buffer attachments; a compact `r16uint` card-ID target is written in a second depth-equal pass so the G-buffer stays within WebGPU's default MRT byte limit. Packed position alpha carries stable per-object visibility IDs; temporal reuse validates history by object ID, previous position, and normal. Stable surfaces update in rotating checkerboard halves. Moving vehicle surfaces and ground receivers bypass radiance accumulation and refresh every frame while the vehicle moves; stable walls still use motion-aware reprojection. The per-pixel screen-space motion vector is derived from the previous camera and vehicle transforms without another full-resolution attachment. A 5×5 edge-aware spatial denoiser follows the temporal resolve. Static non-emissive city quads map to 4×4 world-space mesh-card radiance tiles; oversized quads are subdivided into patches no longer than 64 m per edge to keep the cache spatially local. Only visible cards without a valid cache generation are shaded, in bounded batches. Cached emissive-area diffuse lighting and multi-bounce GI are reused independent of camera motion, while primary sun and optional point-light visibility stay live; a 24 m vehicle-influence zone remains on the per-pixel path with reactive temporal history. The screen-reprojected temporal cache remains for cache misses and dynamic surfaces. This is a compact per-quad cache, not Lumen’s full mesh-card allocator or lighting scheduler.
- Adds six sampled warm/cool emissive streetlight panels as actual finite-area lights, with additional unsampled street and traffic signals for the denser city. A Walker alias table selects sampled emitter triangles in constant time per sample, weighted by emitted power; each selected triangle is sampled over its area and checked with an SDF visibility ray. Eight default stratified emissive-area samples per directly shaded pixel (adjustable from 1 to 16) average binary visibility across each finite panel, producing soft penumbrae from the emitter geometry itself. GI-path direct lighting also samples the panels. The optional analytic point light is independent, has a finite spherical source-radius control, and is off by default so emissive panels lead the lighting showcase.
- Splits direct-light sampling, multi-bounce diffuse transport, temporal surface-cache resolve, and final composition into separate WGSL pipelines. Exposes controls for temporal reuse, GI strength and rays, diffuse bounce count, sky fill, distance-field AO, sun intensity/angular radius, optional point-light intensity/source radius, emissive intensity, emitting-surface size, and emissive area-sample count. HDR radiance stays in `rgba16float` through temporal/spatial denoising and tone mapping, with final-output dithering to reduce visible color banding.
- Includes a default-on camera-space wireframe overlay for the three clipmap bounds. The influence render mode colors surfaces by the actual cascade blend; the separate 2D distance-field slice is not shown in the streamlined UI. Drive with **W/A/S/D** or the arrow keys; **Space** brakes. Drag to orbit the camera, use the wheel to zoom, and press **R** or select **Reset** to return to the start.
- When the adapter exposes WebGPU's optional `timestamp-query` feature, the right control panel shows asynchronous per-pass GPU timings for GDF and surface-cache updates, rasterization, lighting, temporal resolve, denoising, and presentation. Select **COPY LOG** to copy up to 120 recent sampled frames as CSV. The displayed pass sum excludes queue idle and CPU time, so it is **not** end-to-end frame time. The FPS counter is requestAnimationFrame cadence, separate from GPU timing; adapters without timestamp-query show timing as unsupported.

## Unreal Engine comparison

This experiment now adds a camera-independent, world-space cache for static city quads: each card stores a 4×4 radiance grid, and visible-card updates are amortized across frames. It is a compact custom surface cache, not Lumen's full mesh-card allocator, scene capture, card-selection strategy, or lighting scheduler; screen-reprojected history remains supplemental for cache misses and dynamic surfaces. The alias table remains the bounded multi-emitter sampler for emissive geometry, and the separate analytic point light exposes a source-radius penumbra control. This is **not** a source-compatible or pixel-identical Unreal Engine implementation.

## Checks

```sh
node Exhibits/DistanceFieldGI/tests/verify-scene.mjs
```

The check validates GLB parsing, Project Drive triangle/material counts, per-tyre raster identity and dimensions, wheelbase, the 64³ × 3 clipmap budget, finite mesh bounds, BVH escape links, all streamed static-world triangles and packed material/card/orientation metadata, deterministic city streaming and worker buffer transfer, the 100 km × 10 km world constants, and the retained analytic-fallback/emissive-sampling budgets. It requires only Node.js and the checked-in asset.
